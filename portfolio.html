the bridge

Where content strategy and visual design meet

Most of my career has been spent structuring content at scale — metadata, taxonomies, publishing workflows, editorial standards. The design work below grew directly out of that: posters and decks built to communicate the same operational priorities I manage in my day job, just in a different register. The throughline in both is the same question — what does this audience actually need to understand, and what's the clearest way to say it?

01

Peak Communication Poster

Amazon Retail & Amazon Business · Canva

Internal poster announcing Phase 2 of a festive sale window during peak season. Built to be read in seconds by teams juggling high-volume operational updates — a single message (Phase 2 is live), a familiar festive frame, and enough visual energy to cut through a crowded internal comms channel.

02

Content Systems in Motion

Amazon Retail & Amazon Business · Canva, PartyRock · Presented to Leadership

Two connected pieces built for the same problem: getting customer service associates through difficult, high-emotion calls consistently. SAGe (SOP Auto-Generation Engine) is a tool I built to auto-generate standard operating procedure content for CSAs, cutting the time to produce a new SOP by roughly 75%. Alongside it, I designed an animated escalation-scenario walkthrough — a customer disputing a refund, played out step by step — and presented both to the leadership team as a single case for how AI-assisted content tools and clearer training material reduce handling time together.

03

Proofreading Guide

Internshala · Canva · Session deck

A 13-slide checklist-style guide built for a content team, walking through why proofreading matters and how to do it systematically — grammar, structure, flow, SEO impact. Designed with consistent illustration and a clear numbered structure so it could double as a reference doc, not just a one-time session.

04

"Mistakes That Should Scare You"

Internshala · Canva · Live session deck, 400+ attendees

Built from scratch — content and design both — for a live session run for an audience of 400+. A seasonal Halloween frame around a genuinely useful topic: the content mistakes that quietly kill engagement. The theme is a hook, not a gimmick — it's used consistently through every slide so the humor doesn't undercut the substance.

05

One India, My India

Independent project, for a school teacher · Animated data piece

An Independence Day piece made independently for a school teacher, built to teach students about India's linguistic diversity — a flag unfurls to reveal the outline of the country, then the map resolves into its major language regions, each script appearing in its own hand. Built entirely from scratch, including the content and the research behind it.

Full animation available on request — shown here as key frames.
06

Weekly Newsletter

Internshala · Figma · Weekly, ongoing

A weekly newsletter for students and subscribers, designed and laid out in Figma — my first production design work in the tool the role calls for directly. Built as a reusable structure (header, article blocks, a highlight section) so each issue could go out on a consistent weekly cadence without rebuilding the layout from scratch.
